# Worked examples

These show the expected depth and voice. Adapt the wording to the user. Don't copy it.

---

## 1. "I can't do this relationship anymore" (text)

**Situation:** Dating ~3 months. She texts: "hey, I've been thinking a lot and I don't think I can do this relationship anymore. I'm sorry." User is hurt and wants to know what to say.

**Reading it:** This is a decision, not an opening to negotiate. "I've been thinking a lot" means it's been building for a while. "I'm sorry" signals guilt, and she's probably bracing for a bad reaction.
**From their side:** She's dreading this moment. A calm, kind reply will be a relief and will leave a good last impression. Arguing or pleading will make her more sure, and she may stop replying.
**Your goal here:** Self-respect and how this ends. You can't win the objective (staying together) by text, and trying will cost the other two.

**Option A — Gracious and honest** (recommended)
> that's really hard to read but I appreciate you telling me straight. I've genuinely enjoyed being with you. I'm not going to try to change your mind, take care of yourself ok?

Gets you: you leave with your head up, and the door isn't slammed. · Risks: no closure on *why*.

**Option B — One gentle ask for clarity**
> I appreciate you being honest. it stings, not gonna lie. if you're open to it, I'd like to understand what changed, but totally ok if you'd rather not get into it

Gets you: a chance at closure. · Risks: she may not want to explain; you must accept "I'd rather not."

**Option C — Ask for space**
> ok. I need a little time to process this. I'll reach out if I want to talk

Gets you: time before you say something you regret. · Risks: can read a bit cold.

**Before you send:** wait at least 30–60 minutes if you're upset. Don't send paragraphs, ask "is there someone else", or text again tonight. If she replies with an explanation, thank her and don't debate it. If she says she'd like to stay friends, you don't have to decide now: "maybe down the line, I just need some space first."

## 2. Coffee chat went well, but no referral (LinkedIn)

**Situation:** Had a 20-minute chat with an analyst at a bank last week. Good conversation, sent a thank-you, and they replied "happy to help!" No mention of a referral. The application closes in 10 days.

**Reading it:** "Happy to help!" is warm but generic. They weren't refusing; you just never asked directly. Most people won't offer a referral unprompted.
**From their side:** A referral puts their name on you after one conversation. They'll say yes more easily if it's specific (a role link), low-effort (a blurb they can paste), and comes with an easy out.
**Your goal here:** The objective (a referral) without hurting the relationship.

**Option A — Direct, easy ask** (recommended)
> Hi [Name], thanks again for the chat last week. Your advice on [specific thing] was really helpful. I'm applying for the [Role] position ([link]), which closes on the 28th. Would you be comfortable referring me? Here's a quick blurb in case it helps: "[2-line pitch]." Totally understand if it's not the right fit!

Gets you: a clear yes or no before the deadline. · Risks: a small chance it feels fast. The easy out softens that.

**Option B — Ask for direction**
> Hi [Name], thanks again for last week! I'm applying for [Role]. Is there anyone on that team you'd suggest I reach out to, or any advice for standing out in the process?

Gets you: an easier yes, and sometimes they offer the referral themselves. · Risks: slower, and it may not produce a referral.

**Option C — Nurture first**
> Hi [Name], quick update: I took your advice and [did X]. Really appreciated it. Hope your week's going well!

Gets you: relationship capital for later. · Risks: too slow for a 10-day deadline.

**Before you send:** send on a weekday morning. Don't send your resume unless they ask, and don't follow up within 3 days. If they decline or say they can't, thank them warmly and keep the relationship ("totally understand, thanks for the advice either way"). If there's no reply in a week, send one short nudge, then move on and network elsewhere.

## 3. Professor says no to an extension (email)

**Situation:** User asked for 3 more days on a project. The professor replied: "Unfortunately I can't grant extensions for this assignment per the syllabus. Please submit what you have by the deadline."

**Reading it:** A firm, policy-based no. It's not personal, and "submit what you have" hints that partial work gets credit.
**From their side:** They have to be fair to every student. Pushing back makes them defend the policy and remember you as difficult. A gracious reply makes them remember you as mature, which matters for later favors.
**Your goal here:** Relationship and self-respect, plus getting as many points as possible (the objective has changed).

**Option A — Accept and get useful info** (recommended)
> Subject: Re: Extension request — [Course] Project 2
>
> Dear Professor [Last name],
>
> Understood, thank you for the quick reply. I'll submit what I have by the deadline. Would it be okay to stop by office hours on [day] for 10 minutes to make sure I'm prioritizing the right sections?
>
> Best,
> [Full name], [Course + section]

Gets you: goodwill plus help maximizing your grade. · Risks: none really.

**Option B — Simple acknowledgment**
> Understood. Thank you, Professor [Last name]. I'll submit by the deadline.

Gets you: clean and respectful. · Risks: misses a chance to get help.

**Only if there's a real, documented emergency** (illness, family emergency, disability accommodation): don't argue with the professor. Contact the dean of students or disability services office, who can handle it formally.

**Before you send:** reply the same day. Don't re-explain your reasons or mention other students who got extensions.

## 4. Manager: "This was due yesterday. Where is it?" (Slack)

**Reading it:** Frustration, plus pressure from above. They need a reliable ETA more than a reason.
**From their side:** They're worried about looking bad to their own boss. What calms them: ownership, a specific time, and no surprises.
**Your goal here:** Restore trust (relationship) and deliver (objective).

**Option A — Own it plus ETA** (recommended)
> You're right, that's on me. I'll have it to you by 3pm today. The last piece is the [X] section. Want me to send what's done now so you have something for [their meeting]?

Gets you: trust and a fix. · Risks: you must hit 3pm.

**Option B — Own it plus flag a priority conflict** (if they also gave you other urgent work)
> That's on me. I should have flagged it earlier. I got pulled into [Y]. I can finish this by 3pm today if I pause Y. Does that work, or is Y the priority?

Gets you: shares the trade-off honestly. · Risks: sounds like an excuse if Y wasn't from them.

**Before you send:** reply within minutes, not hours. Don't write a paragraph of excuses. If you'll miss the new time, say so *before* it passes.

## 5. Hinge match replies with only "haha"

**Reading it:** Low effort, but not necessarily disinterest. They might be busy, or your last message didn't give them much to reply to. If it's the third short reply in a row, it's likely a fade.
**From their side:** They're skimming several chats. They reply to things that are easy and fun to answer.
**Your goal here:** Find out if there's interest without over-investing.

**Option A — Give them something easy and fun to answer** (recommended)
> ok real question: [specific question tied to their profile, e.g. "best taco spot in the city, go"]

**Option B — Skip ahead to a plan**
> I feel like this would be more fun in person. want to grab coffee this week?

**Option C — Let it breathe:** don't reply now. If they re-engage, pick it up.

**Before you send:** if two more messages get one-word replies, it's a polite fade. Let it go without comment.

## 6. Commenting on a client's LinkedIn post (the client announced a funding round)

**Reading it / their side:** They want visibility and warm wishes. A thoughtful comment from a partner looks good to *their* network too.
**Option A — Specific congrats** (recommended)
> Congrats to the whole [Company] team! Watching you grow [specific thing, e.g. "from 3 to 30 stores"] has been awesome. Excited for what's next.

**Option B — Congrats + light insight**
> Huge milestone. [One specific observation about why it's deserved]. Well earned, [Name]!

**Don't:** pitch your services, or write "Congrats!!" with nothing else.
