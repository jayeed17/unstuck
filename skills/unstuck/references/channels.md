# Channel and platform norms

Match the platform. A good message on the wrong platform still reads wrong.

## Dating apps (Hinge, Tinder, Bumble, Raya)

- Short: 1–2 sentences early on. Match their length.
- Openers: personal and specific to their profile (a prompt, a photo detail), and easy to answer. Avoid "hey", "hi beautiful", or a pickup line copied off the internet. Hinge's own data found personalized first messages get far more engagement, and different ages respond to different kinds of openers.
- A question or a playful "hook" gives them something to reply to. A wall of questions feels like an interview.
- Comment on something they chose to show (a prompt, a hobby), not their body.
- Move toward a plan within roughly 5–15 back-and-forths if it's going well: a specific, low-pressure invite ("want to grab a drink at X Thursday?") beats "we should hang sometime".
- Emoji and lowercase are fine if the user texts that way.
- No double-texting after an unanswered message within a day. One light follow-up after a few days max, then let it go.

## Texting (iMessage, WhatsApp, SMS, Instagram DM)

- Mirror their style: length, punctuation, emoji, lowercase.
- Serious topics (breakups, big conflicts, apologies for big things): suggest a call or in-person talk, and use text to set it up ("can we talk tonight? want to do it properly, not over text").
- Several short texts in a row is normal between close friends; with a newer person, one message is safer.
- A period at the end of a short text can read cold to younger people. Notice the user's voice.
- Voice notes: suggest them only if the user and the other person already use them.

## LinkedIn

- Connection note: under 300 characters. Who you are, the one specific reason you're reaching out, and nothing else.
- DMs: 3–5 short sentences. Specific reference (their post, their path, a shared school or program), one clear and small ask ("15 minutes", "one question").
- No attachments or resumes in a first message unless asked.
- Follow up once after about a week if there's no reply, then stop or move on.
- Thank-yous within 24 hours, and specific.
- Comments on posts: add something (a real takeaway, a question, a short experience). Avoid "Great post!" or "Congrats!!" alone, and don't pitch yourself in the comments.

## Email (professors, managers, clients, recruiters)

- **Subject line** says exactly what it is: "Extension request: CIS 3920 Project 2", "Q3 report — revised timeline".
- **Greeting**: professors default to "Dear Professor [Last name]" or "Hi Professor [Last name]" unless they've signaled otherwise. Managers and clients: match how they write to you.
- **BLUF**: the point or ask in the first two lines.
- Short paragraphs, under ~150 words for most requests.
- One ask, with a deadline or proposed options if relevant.
- Sign-off with full name; students add the course and section.
- Don't email anything you'd hate to see forwarded.

## Slack and Teams

- Lead with the point; no "hi" followed by waiting for a reply ("no hello" rule).
- Threads for replies; DMs for anything sensitive. Don't call out mistakes in public channels.
- Async-friendly: include enough context that they can answer without asking follow-ups.
- Tone carries less on chat. Add a little warmth on hard messages ("totally get it's been a crazy week").

## Phone and in person

Recommend these when:
- The message would be long, emotional, or easy to misread
- There's been back-and-forth confusion already (3+ messages going in circles)
- Real news is being delivered: ending something, a big apology, bad news to a client
- Power dynamics make text feel cold (asking a manager for something important)

Give a 2–3 line text that sets up the call, plus talking points for the call.

## Timing

- **Hot emotions → wait.** If the user is angry, hurt or panicking, suggest drafting now and sending after a break (an hour, or the next morning).
- **Work and school** during business hours; schedule-send late-night emails.
- **Professors**: ask for extensions before the deadline, ideally several days before.
- **Dating**: no need to play games with fake delays; reply when natural. Don't reply instantly at 3am to a serious message.
- **Follow-ups**: LinkedIn and cold email, ~1 week; after a coffee chat, thanks within 24 hours; recruiters, about a week past the date they gave you.
